lite-binary-reader — layout visuals

Every grid below is generated from the reader's actual schema offsets, field types and stride at runtime — not hand-drawn. Every decoded number is a real reader read. Served with npx serve demo; imports only ../Reader.js.

1. laneOf() → GPU: the zero-copy hand-off

An interleaved vertex buffer (AoS, stride 20) is exactly what a mesh loader or GPU readback hands you. laneOf() proves it is host-endian and aligned — i.e. uploadable as-is — and yields the exact gl.vertexAttribPointer arithmetic. The canvas uploads reader.buffer straight to bufferData: no JS-side repack, no unpack loop, no allocation.

The same bytes, drawn by WebGL. Position is a vec3 of 3 consecutive f32 fields; color a normalized vec4 of 4 u8 fields — composed from scalar lanes, contiguity verified.

2. The unaligned big-endian read — and the padding reframe

A foreign wire record (stride 8): u8 kind@0, f32 value@1 (unaligned), u16 seq@5, big-endian. A Float32Array cannot begin at byte 1; the reader can. Toggle how the 4 value-bytes are interpreted — it is a real read through a reader built for that endianness.

The padding reframe

The usual "wasted bytes in red" trope does not fit this library — its tight layouts often have no padding. The true, reader-specific contrast: exposing value as a Float32Array lane would REQUIRE padding it to a 4-aligned offset (red gap bytes). The reader reads the tight, unaligned layout with no gaps at all.
